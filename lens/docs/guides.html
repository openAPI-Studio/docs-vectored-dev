Guides and Guide Mode

Record a click-by-click walkthrough, edit it, share it, and play it back on the live page.

A guide is a numbered sequence of steps, each with its own screenshot and the control it points at. Lens records one by watching your clicks — there is no region to drag and no video behind it.

1. Recording one

Press Smart Capture in the popup, or its keyboard shortcut. A briefing appears first, explaining how the recorder behaves; nothing is recorded until you press Start recording, and Cancel leaves nothing behind.

  1. Press Smart Capture the briefing opens over the page.
  2. Read it and press Start recording a small bar appears in the corner with the step count, Finish and discard.
  3. Work through your flow at a normal, unhurried pace. Every click on a real control becomes a numbered step.
  4. Press Finish the guide is assembled and offered for saving into the active project.

Four things decide whether a guide comes out right, and the briefing says all four:

RuleWhy
Leave about a second between clicksChrome allows a tab to be photographed about twice a second. Click faster and the step is still recorded, it just has no picture.
Pause after typing before clicking onWhat you type becomes one step, written down about a second and a half after you stop — not one step per key.
Keep the recorded tab in frontA tab that is not the visible one cannot be photographed. Following a link into a new tab is fine; the walkthrough goes with it.
Press and release in one placeA drag, or a press held longer than about a second and a half, is read as a text selection and dropped.

Clicking into a text box, or opening a dropdown, is deliberately not a step. The step is what you typed, or the option you picked — not the click that got you there. Recording stops at 60 steps.

The corner bar never appears in the pictures. Turn the briefing off for good from the dialog itself, or from Dashboard → Settings → Explain Smart Capture before it starts.

2. Editing a guide

Dashboard → Guides → Open editor. Each step has its wording, its screenshot and its own controls: reorder, duplicate, split a run of clicks, add a note below the picture, or delete it. The header block carries the title, author, status, date, step count, how long the guide takes and a comments field — all of it editable, and all of it carried into every export.

The time is worked out from the steps unless you type your own figure, and the note beside it is yours to write: say *including approvals* and that is what the exports say.

Markdown at the top of the ribbon swaps the formatted view for the guide's raw Markdown. Edits made there — including in the header table and the comments — come back with you.

3. Exporting

Dashboard → Guides → Export offers four destinations:

ExportWhat it is for
Share bundle (.zip)One file to send to a colleague. Downloads.
Self-contained HTMLOne guide.html with every picture inside it. Written into the project folder.
Markdownguide.md beside its screenshots, for a repo or a docs site.
Copy to clipboardRich text for Confluence, Notion or a doc.

Export all guides, beside the Import button, writes every guide into one archive.

4. Sharing a guide with a colleague

Share bundle is the one to send. It holds the guide itself — the steps and the control each one points at — its screenshots, and readable HTML and Markdown copies for anyone who only wants to read it. Your colleague imports it from Dashboard → Guides → Import, and it arrives as a guide with a Play button, not a document about one.

Import also takes a link, so a bundle put on an internal share can be pulled in by address rather than by file.

A shared guide plays with rings only against the same screens. Guide Mode finds each step's control by a selector recorded against the page you captured on, so a colleague on the same app and version gets the rings in the right places, and one on a different build may not. The pictures and the words are unaffected either way.

5. Printing a guide, or saving it as PDF

From the editor, Export → Print / Save as PDF. The guide prints on its own — no toolbars, no side panels, no per-step buttons — as one continuous strip sized to the document rather than sliced into A4 pages, so no screenshot is ever cut in half. A guide long enough to exceed a single PDF page breaks between steps, never through a picture.

6. Guide Mode — playing a guide back

Play on any guide card runs it against the live page. Each step is shown in turn with its control ringed on the real page, so the reader follows along in the software rather than reading about it.

A walkthrough recorded with Interactive walkthrough turned off records the screenshots and the words only, with nothing about the page's own markup. It reads and exports perfectly, and it does not play.

7. Where guides live

Each guide is a folder inside its project, holding guide.json and its screenshots. A guide is listed because project_metadata.json names it — copying a folder in by hand achieves nothing on its own, which is what Import is for.
